Overview

Voice assistants that answer a company’s phone the way its best receptionist would: they understand what the caller is asking for, put them through to the right person on the company’s phone system, take messages and answer with the company’s own information, at any hour.

They are in production at many companies, with 33 active assistants on 23 phone lines. To sound natural and respond this fast, every link in the chain runs on best-in-class providers for speech recognition (STT), speech synthesis (TTS) and inference. What makes the difference is the layer of my own that coordinates them.

The challenge

Voice is unforgiving. In a text chat, two seconds of thinking is fine; on a phone call it feels like the line has dropped. Every component, from speech recognition to the language model to speech synthesis, eats into a tight latency budget.

Language models are probabilistic; a switchboard is not. Who a call is transferred to, the exact wording used to confirm a message or how a phone number is read back cannot be left to chance.

Architecture

Key decisions

  1. 01

    An intelligence layer of my own

    The heart of the system is an orchestration layer I designed to sit between the voice platform and the language model. Instead of blindly trusting whatever the AI generates, it supervises it in real time.

  2. 02

    It enforces the steps of the process

    If the model tries to skip confirming a message, or to ask again for something the caller has already said, the layer corrects it before the caller hears it.

  3. 03

    Deterministic rules where failure is not an option

    Who a call is transferred to, the exact sentence used to confirm a message and how a phone number is read out are not left to the model’s chance.

  4. 04

    It saves time on every turn

    When the answer is obvious, such as a “yes” to a confirmation, it acts without waiting for the model.

  5. 05

    Highly available and provider-independent

    It works with several AI providers at once and fails over automatically if one fails or degrades. The model can be changed without touching a single assistant.

Validated with data

I built a test bench that replays real calls with a simulated caller and a blind automated judge. Using 701 calls from a real week, I pitted my architecture against the voice platform’s native features: guided flows, procedures and their hosted models, alone and combined.

What it can do

  • Phone system integration

    Transfers to extensions, ring groups and mobiles. If nobody answers, it tries and offers to take a message without the caller noticing the switch.

  • Smart messages

    It reads messages back to confirm them and delivers them by email or WhatsApp to the right person, depending on who was being called or which line the call came in on.

  • Opening hours and holidays

    Automatic out-of-hours mode, with a holiday calendar and custom announcements for each line.

  • Languages and company knowledge

    Several languages and each company’s own information: services, opening hours, frequently asked questions.

  • Natural conversation

    Phrasing designed from real calls, so it does not sound like a robot.

A platform for the business

  • Express onboarding

    A new customer is set up in minutes from a sales questionnaire.

  • Customer portal

    Each company sees its calls, transcripts, messages and usage, and receives automatic PDF reports, with manager and employee roles.

  • Cost control

    Real usage per customer, minute limits and alerts.

  • Enterprise-grade security

    Customer data isolated in the database itself, two-factor authentication, verified external audits and backup-restore drills.

  • Robust operations

    Continuous monitoring with alerts, an automatic daily review of calls, and deployments that only run with every test green and no call in progress.
